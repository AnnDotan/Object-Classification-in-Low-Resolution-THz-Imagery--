\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Background.}\quad
Terahertz (THz) imaging sensors produce frames that are simultaneously low
resolution, low signal-to-noise, and colorless. ImageNet-pretrained classifiers,
which rely on fine textures and saturated chroma, lose accuracy quickly under
those conditions, but the magnitude and the mechanism of the loss are not
documented quantitatively in the literature.

\noindent\textbf{Aim.}\quad
Measure how three representative deep architectures (a residual CNN, a densely
connected CNN, and a foveal vision transformer) degrade under controlled
synthetic THz-like degradation, decompose the loss into per-axis contributions,
and quantify how much of it can be recovered by training-time regularization.

\noindent\textbf{Methods.}\quad
The 402-cell campaign trains ResNet50, DenseNet121 and TransNeXt-tiny on
CIFAR-10 and MNIST under a five-level degradation curve (saturation,
downsample, blur, additive noise, salt and pepper). Seven phases isolate the
effects: A clean baseline, B combined, C single-axis, D regularization
recovery, B2 / B2-nr THz protocol simplification, and C2 single-axis under the
THz protocol. A 48-replicate multi-seed audit ($\{42, 43, 44\}$) on the 24 L3
headline cells bounds reproducibility. Every degradation pipeline is anchored
to a deterministic 256-image PSNR / SSIM probe so the cross-phase comparison
sits on a physical scale.

\noindent\textbf{Findings.}\quad
On CIFAR-10 the combined L3 cell costs roughly 12.5 \Mpp{} of accuracy
relative to the clean baseline; regularization (T3 in Phase D) recovers
$\sim$0.77 \Mpp{} of that drop. Resolution dominates the per-axis attribution
by a factor of $\sim$4 (mean L1 to L5 effect: $-27.45$ \Mpp{} vs $-6.58$ for
blur and $-5.13$ for salt and pepper). The multi-seed audit confirms
$\sigma < 1.5$ \Mpp{} on every L3 headline cell. Expected calibration error
inflates from below 5 \Mpp{} at L3 to above 15 \Mpp{} at L5 on CIFAR-10:
confidence is preserved even as the spatial signal collapses.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusions.}\quad
The Phase B accuracy collapse is best modeled as an information bottleneck,
not as an overfit. The B2 / B2-nr decomposition shows that THz-protocol
simplification recovers $\sim$3.24 \Mpp{} of pure-protocol headroom on top of
the regularization recovery. Single-axis attribution implies that any THz
deployment should invest in resolution restoration before denoising or
deblurring.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.}\quad
object classification, deep learning, low resolution, THz imaging, robustness,
PSNR, SSIM, calibration, transformer, residual network, dense network.
