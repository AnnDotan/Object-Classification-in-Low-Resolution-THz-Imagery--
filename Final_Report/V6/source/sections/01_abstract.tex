\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Background.}\quad
Terahertz (THz) cameras can see through clothing, paper, and plastic, which
makes them attractive for security screening and medical imaging. The physics
of the band, however, exacts a price: the images come out at very low
resolution, noisy, and without color. Deep-learning classifiers are pretrained
on sharp, colorful photographs, and it has not been documented quantitatively
how much accuracy they lose under THz-like conditions, which kind of image
degradation causes most of the damage, or whether smarter training can win the
loss back.

\noindent\textbf{Aim.}\quad
Measure the robustness of three representative deep architectures --- a
residual CNN (ResNet50), a densely connected CNN (DenseNet121), and a vision
transformer (TransNeXt-tiny) --- under controlled synthetic THz-like
degradation; attribute the accuracy loss to the individual degradation types;
and quantify how much of it training-time regularization recovers.

\noindent\textbf{Methods.}\quad
A 402-run training campaign covers the three models on CIFAR-10 and MNIST
across five severity levels (L1 mild to L5 extreme) and seven experimental
phases: clean baselines, all degradations combined (Phase B1), one degradation
at a time, a regularization-recovery sweep, and a simplified THz protocol
(grayscale, no additive noise) with its own single-axis sweep (Phase C2).
This report analyses Phases B1 and C2 in full and summarizes the remaining
phases briefly. Every degradation pipeline is anchored to measured PSNR and
SSIM values, so degradation types are compared on the image quality they
actually produce rather than on the preset severity index; a 48-run
multi-seed audit bounds the run-to-run noise.

\noindent\textbf{Findings.}\quad
Four results carry the campaign. (1)~The transformer is the most robust model
in the mild-to-moderate range: on CIFAR-10 it leads the two CNNs by roughly
10~\Mpp{} at levels L1--L2. (2)~At extreme degradation the architecture stops
mattering --- all three models converge into a 19--21\% accuracy band on
CIFAR-10. (3)~Resolution loss is by far the most damaging degradation type:
at matched image quality it costs about four times more accuracy than blur or
salt-and-pepper noise. (4)~Regularization recovers less than one percentage
point of the roughly 12.5-point combined-degradation collapse, so the loss
reflects information that was destroyed, not a model that over-fitted.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusions.}\quad
For THz-like systems operating at mild-to-moderate degradation, the vision
transformer is the recommended backbone. At extreme degradation no classifier
choice helps, because the discriminative detail is no longer in the image; the
practical leverage there is restoring spatial resolution ahead of the
classifier, before any denoising or deblurring.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.}\quad
object classification, deep learning, low resolution, THz imaging,
robustness, PSNR, SSIM, calibration, transformer, residual network,
dense network.
