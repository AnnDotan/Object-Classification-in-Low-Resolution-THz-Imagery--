\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:tests}

This section reports the measured performance against every relevant metric.
Every value comes from a frozen training run recorded under
\texttt{runs/final/} and aggregated by \texttt{artifacts/Final\_Exp.json}
(schema version 3). No values are synthesized or interpolated. In line with
the focus of this revision, the two phases that carry the headline science ---
Phase B1 (combined degradation) and Phase C2 (single-axis isolation under the
THz protocol) --- are reported in full, with tables, figures, and a
model-by-model reading. The remaining phases are summarized qualitatively in
Section~\ref{sec:tests:brief}; their complete per-cell numbers live in the
aggregated artifacts referenced in the Appendix.

\subsection{The remaining phases in brief}
\label{sec:tests:brief}
The phases outside the B1 / C2 focus are summarized here qualitatively; they
provide context and bounds for the two detailed analyses that follow.

\sectheading{Clean baselines (Phase A).} All three backbones classify the
undegraded validation sets at near-ceiling accuracy, establishing the upper
bound against which every degraded cell is measured.

\sectheading{Full single-axis isolation (Phase C).} Sweeping all five
degradation axes one at a time confirms that spatial resolution is the dominant
driver of accuracy loss, whereas desaturation and salt-and-pepper noise are
comparatively benign in isolation. This ordering motivated the THz-relevant
three-axis subset that is analysed in depth as Phase C2.

\sectheading{Regularization recovery (Phase D).} Layering dropout, mix-up and
cut-mix on top of the combined-degradation pipeline recovers only a small
fraction of the accuracy lost to degradation. The collapse is therefore an
information bottleneck, not an over-fitting artefact, and cannot be closed by
regularization alone.

\sectheading{THz protocol simplification (Phase B2 / B2-nr).} Reducing the
pipeline to the THz-relevant operators --- removing the colour-desaturation and
additive-noise axes --- yields a modest, consistent accuracy margin over the
full combined pipeline. Decomposing that margin shows it is driven mostly by
the protocol simplification itself rather than by regularization.

\sectheading{Multi-seed audit.} Re-running the headline cells under additional
random seeds leaves the reported accuracies essentially unchanged: run-to-run
variance is small relative to every effect discussed below, so the comparisons
are robust to seed choice.

\sectheading{Calibration.} The networks grow over-confident as the spatial
information collapses --- predicted confidence increasingly exceeds empirical
accuracy at the most severe levels. Regularization and protocol simplification
reduce but do not eliminate this miscalibration.

\sectheading{Inference throughput.} Inference latency meets the real-time
budget for the convolutional backbones; the transformer backbone is somewhat
slower because its attention path is not graph-compiled on the current stack.

\sectheading{Confusion structure.} At the most severe level the residual errors
concentrate among visually similar classes, indicating the networks retain
coarse category structure even after fine detail is destroyed.

\subsection{Phase B1: combined degradation}
\label{sec:tests:phase_b1}
Phase B1 applies all five degradation axes simultaneously at a common level.
Table~\ref{tab:phase_b} reports the full $3 \times 2 \times 5$ accuracy block,
and Figure~\ref{fig:b1_curves} plots each model's accuracy against the combined
level, with the Phase A clean baseline drawn as a dotted line per model. Here
the level index is a legitimate $x$-axis: Phase B1 sweeps a single fixed
combined recipe, so each level denotes one well-defined operating point.

On CIFAR-10 accuracy declines monotonically from the mild level (L1) to the
extreme level (L5). TransNeXt-tiny leads at the mild and moderate end
($87.58\,\%$ at L1, against a CNN mean near $77\,\%$), but the three
architectures converge into a tight $[19,21]\,\%$ band at L5, where the spatial
information has effectively been removed. MNIST is far more resilient: all
three backbones stay above $98\,\%$ at L1 and only fall to roughly
$27$--$28\,\%$ at L5. The model-by-model reading of this convergence is given
in Section~\ref{sec:crossmodel}.

\subsection{Phase C2: single-axis under the THz protocol}
\label{sec:tests:phase_c2}
Phase C2 isolates each of the three THz-relevant degradation axes ---
resolution, blur, and salt-and-pepper --- under the simplified protocol
(saturation and additive noise disabled), so each cell varies exactly one
operator. Table~\ref{tab:phase_c2} reports the accuracy block.

\input{tables/tab_IX_phase_c2.tex}

A given nominal level does not impose the same image quality on different
axes: L3 blur and L3 resolution are not equally destructive. Comparing the
axes on the L1--L5 index would therefore be meaningless --- the level is a
predetermined parameter, not a measure of how much the image was actually
degraded. We instead plot accuracy against the \emph{measured} image quality,
PSNR and SSIM, computed against the clean upsampled reference.

Figure~\ref{fig:c2_type} compares the three axes on a common PSNR and SSIM
scale, with one curve per axis averaged over the three models. At matched image
quality, resolution degradation is by far the most harmful: its curve sits well
below blur and salt-and-pepper at every PSNR and SSIM value, while
salt-and-pepper is nearly flat. The Spearman rank correlation confirms that
resolution carries the strongest accuracy--quality association
($\rho = +0.98$ against both metrics on CIFAR-10).
